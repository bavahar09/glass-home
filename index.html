Which TV do I have?

The brand on the TV isn't enough: a TCL or Hisense TV can run Roku, Google TV, Fire TV or VIDAA. The remote (or the screen you see when the TV starts) tells you which. Pick the one that matches:

“fire tv” on the remoteor a blue microphone (Alexa) button; the home screen says “fire tv”Amazon Fire TV → “Roku” on the remoteoften purple buttons; the home screen is a grid of app tiles with a purple menu on the leftRoku → A Google Assistant button(a microphone or four coloured dots); the TV shows “Google TV” or “Android TV” when it startsGoogle TV / Android TV → “SAMSUNG” on the remotethe home screen is a row of apps along the bottomSamsung → “LG” on the remoteoften a pointer you move by waving the remote (Magic Remote)LG → A small black box with a slim remotesilver or black, with a touch surface or ring at the topApple TV → “VIDAA”, Vizio, or something elseany TV with a web browserAnother TV →

Still not sure? Open the TV's Settings → About (or System → About): it names the system, like Fire OS, Roku OS, Google TV, Android TV, Tizen (Samsung), webOS (LG) or VIDAA. If a streaming stick or box is plugged into the TV and that's what you watch on, pick the stick.

Amazon Fire TV

Fire TV Stick, Fire TV Cube, and TVs with Fire TV built in

  1. On the Fire TV, open Find → Search, type Downloader, and install the orange Downloader app.
  2. Allow it to install apps: Settings → My Fire TV → Developer options → Install unknown apps → Downloader → On.
    No Developer options? Go to Settings → My Fire TV → About, press OK on the device name 7 times, then go back.
  3. Open Downloader, choose the address box and type this page's address: this page
  4. On this page in Downloader, press Install Glass Home below → Install → Open.

Optional: open it with the Home button

Fire TV only allows this from a computer, once. It also turns on ≡ ≡ (screen off, sound on) and ≡ ≡ ≡ (video in a widget).

  1. On the Fire TV, turn on Settings → My Fire TV → Developer options → ADB debugging, and open Glass Home.
  2. On a Mac or Windows PC on the same Wi-Fi, open this page and run the helper. It finds the TV by itself.

    Mac: open Terminal (Spotlight: ⌘ Space, type Terminal), paste this and press Return:

    bash <(curl -fsSL THIS-PAGE/home-button/mac.sh)

    Or download GlassHome-HomeButton-Mac.zip and double-click the file inside. If the Mac says it can't check it: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

    Windows: open PowerShell (Start, type PowerShell), paste this and press Enter:

    irm THIS-PAGE/home-button/windows.ps1 | iex

    Or download GlassHome-HomeButton-Windows.bat and double-click it. If Windows warns: More info → Run anyway.

  3. When the TV asks to allow the computer, choose Always allow from this computer, then OK.

The helper gets Android's adb tool from Google if the computer doesn't have it, and changes only Glass Home's Home-button permission on the TV. Glass Home's setup screen turns ✓ by itself. Afterwards you can turn ADB debugging off again.

Doing it by hand with adb 
adb connect FIRE-TV-ADDRESS
adb shell settings put secure enabled_accessibility_services com.bavahar.glass/com.bavahar.glass.HomeWatcher
adb shell settings put secure accessibility_enabled 1
adb shell pm grant com.bavahar.glass android.permission.WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS

The address is in Settings → My Fire TV → About → Network. If another accessibility service is on (e.g. VoiceView), add Glass Home's after it with a colon instead of replacing it.

Roku

Roku TVs, Streaming Stick, Ultra. Needs a computer or phone for the upload

  1. On the Roku remote press Home ×3, Up ×2, Right, Left, Right, Left, Right. Turn on Developer Mode and choose a password. The TV shows an address like http://192.168.1.20.
  2. On a computer or phone on the same Wi-Fi, download the Roku file below, then open the TV's address and sign in as rokudev with your password.

Not on Apple TV

Apple TV

Apple TV has no web browser and only runs apps from Apple's App Store, so Glass Home can't run on it (yet).

  1. The TV the Apple TV is plugged into is often a smart TV itself. Use its own remote and look again at which TV it is: Samsung, LG, Roku or Google TV can show Glass Home.
  2. Or add a small streaming stick for the full version, next to the Apple TV.

Full Glass Home on any TV

Want everything, whatever your TV?

Roku, Samsung, LG and other TVs get the clock and weather version. For the full Glass Home (photos, calendar, notes, your phone as a remote, the Home button), plug a streaming stick into any free HDMI port:

  1. Amazon Fire TV Stick (any 4K model), or a Google TV box or stick (onn 4K, Chromecast with Google TV, Google TV Streamer). Usually about $20–50.
  2. Set it up, then follow the Fire TV or Google TV steps on this page.
